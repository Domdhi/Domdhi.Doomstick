Doomstick Field Manual

Public-domain survival and reference corpus — offline, file://

A portable offline reference library covering survival skills, emergency medicine, edible plant identification, amateur radio regulations, well water safety, and practical knots. All content is sourced from US Government works or pre-1928 publications and is in the public domain. No internet connection required.

01 — US Army

Survival Skills — FM 21-76

The S.U.R.V.I.V.A.L. acronym, survival priority order, shelter construction (lean-to, debris hut, snow trench), fire making (bow drill, fire lays), water procurement and purification, signaling techniques, navigation without instruments, food procurement, trapping basics, and field first aid.

Source: US Army FM 21-76, 1992 • US Gov. public domain
02 — FEMA

First Aid & Disaster Response — CERT

Scene safety (STAR), mass casualty triage, START triage algorithm (RPM assessment, four categories), bleeding control (direct pressure, tourniquets, wound packing), shock recognition and treatment, spinal precautions, splinting, burn classification and treatment, airway management, and CERT search and rescue.

Source: FEMA CERT training program • US Gov. public domain
03 — USDA

Edible Wild Plants — Continental US

The 9-step Universal Edibility Test, safe species (dandelion, cattail, wood sorrel, purslane, lamb's quarters, violet, blackberries, wild onion, pine, acorns), and toxic lookalikes to avoid (water hemlock, poison hemlock, death camas, false morel, destroying angel). Preparation and seasonal availability.

Source: USDA ARS & USDA Forest Service • US Gov. public domain
04 — FCC

Amateur Radio — FCC Part 97

Purpose of the amateur radio service, license classes and privileges, call sign structure, key frequency allocations (2m, 70cm, 40m, 20m, 80m), operating rules (§97.119 identification), emergency provisions (§97.401–§97.407, RACES), ARES and SKYWARN, phonetic alphabet, Q codes, and basic antenna theory.

Source: FCC 47 CFR Part 97 • US Gov. public domain
05 — EPA

Private Well Water Safety

Why to test your well, common contaminants and health effects (coliform bacteria, nitrates, lead, arsenic, radon, VOCs), visual/smell/taste indicators, testing schedule and triggers, EPA shock chlorination procedure, emergency water treatment (boiling, bleach, iodine), annual inspection checklist, and finding certified labs.

Source: EPA Private Water Systems guidance • US Gov. public domain
06 — US Navy

Practical Knots

Ten essential knots with ASCII step diagrams: overhand, figure-eight, square knot, sheet bend, bowline ("king of knots"), clove hitch, half hitch and two half hitches, taut-line hitch, rolling hitch, and fisherman's knot. Use case, strength characteristics, and how to untie each.

Source: US Navy seamanship manuals, pre-1928 • Public domain
